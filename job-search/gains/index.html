What you keep

A higher salary elsewhere does not tell you what you gain there. Between gross pay and living standards come tax, prices, the health premium where it sits outside tax, and above all the rent a newcomer pays.

Every amount on this page is a computation made for the study from published figures. Read them within each country first; across countries, only large gaps mean something.

Reading by profile

01

Young graduate, single

The profile for which moving is best supported by research. Compare what is left after an entry rent, not salaries: small flats cost more per square metre (35.3 € in Paris against 17.2 in Rennes). In Canada, mid-sized Québec cities leave more after rent than Toronto or Vancouver.

02

Mid-career, no degree

Flat-rate charges and rent weigh most. At two-thirds of the average wage, an asking rent takes 87% of net pay in Paris, 42 to 44% in Lyon, Marseille or Brussels, and 32% in Wallonia or Brest. Leave expensive metropolitan areas aside, and look at the occupation before the territory.

03

Couple with young children

Cost childcare, health premiums and a family-sized rent before comparing salaries. Net childcare takes 7% of an average wage in Luxembourg, 11% in France and 17% in Switzerland, where premiums for a family of four add 16,000 to 17,700 CHF a year. Outside Québec, French-language schools score well below English-language ones in reading: 69 points lower in Ontario.

04

Aged 50 or more

Moving gives up a housing advantage: in Vaud, a lease of twenty years or more costs 1,092 CHF against 1,673 for a recent one. Move only with a signed job: 43 to 44% of unemployed people aged 55 to 64 have been searching for a year or more in France, Belgium and Switzerland. Pension rights and their portability are not covered here.

05

Newcomer with a foreign diploma

No resident average applies to you. You pay the asking rent, with no seniority. Among graduates born outside the EU, 35% are over-qualified for their job in Belgium, 31% in France, 26% in Switzerland and 14% in Luxembourg. In small francophone communities in Canada, rental markets are tight: 0.7% of dwellings vacant in Hawkesbury, 0.8% in Edmundston.

Where these computations are fragile

  • French cities outside Paris use the national median wage, because no regional median is published. What is left there is an upper bound.
  • Belgian and Luxembourg figures rest on 2022 median wages, carried to net pay between two official points. Luxembourg’s median was read in a summary, not at the source.
  • For Neuchâtel, Fribourg and Sion, the rent is that of sitting tenants: a newcomer pays more. In Geneva, a new tenant pays 18% more for the same size of flat.
  • The Swiss health premium is the average adult premium across all insurance models, with no premium reduction deducted. Refundable tax credits in Canada are not counted.
  • Amounts are in national currency and are not adjusted for prices: compare cities within a country. The first chart of this page still rests on average wages and one tax schedule per federal country.
