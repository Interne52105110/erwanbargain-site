Overall: the score weighs money (what is left after rent) and job (unemployment for your profile and the share of searches lasting a year or more) equally. On each criterion, territories are ranked from best to worst; the score is the average of those positions, scaled to 100. It is a simple rule chosen for this page, not an official index: other weights would give another order. “Find a job easily” ranks on the two job criteria alone. The share of searches lasting a year or more is not published (n/p) for Corsica, Yukon, the Northwest Territories and Nunavut: their job score rests on unemployment alone. The “rent / net pay” column is given for information.

Left each month: our computation, for a single person at the median wage, in the reference city. It is converted into euros at French prices with OECD purchasing power parities (2024): what the amount would buy in France. The amount in local currency is shown underneath. Across countries, read it as an order of magnitude. † weaker basis: rent of sitting tenants, press-reported rent or a different wage definition. In Canada the amount also allows for price differences between cities, housing excluded (Statistics Canada, October 2019): +1% in Whitehorse, +4% in Yellowknife; in Iqaluit only food is measured, so the correction (+16%) is a minimum. For these three territories the figure stays on the favourable side: the wage used is that of people working full time all year.

Province hosting communities open to French-speaking newcomers through a federal programme. Unemployment: Eurostat and Statistics Canada, 2025. The right to work, the language and the occupation come before any ranking. * low reliability · ~ estimated: surveys publish no rate when too few people of that age and education were interviewed in the territory; the cell is then rebuilt from the territory's published rates and the national pattern by age and education.       Long searches vary fivefold

      The share of the unemployed who have been searching for a year or more runs from about 9% in Québec and 12% in Brittany to 37% in Wallonia and the Lake Geneva region, and 46% in Brussels.

      Eurostat 2025; Statistics Canada, computed, Nov 2025–Oct 2026

      Profile outweighs place

      In France, a young person without a diploma faces an unemployment rate eight times that of a graduate aged 55–64. Between French regions, the overall rate only moves from 5.6% to 8.8%.

      Eurostat, lfst_r_lfu3rt, 2025

      24.5%

      After 50, the exit is the problem

      In France, 24.5% of registered jobseekers aged 50 and over found work within six months, against 47.6% of those under 26. Unemployment rates for older workers look low almost everywhere, which hides this.

      France Travail, note 26.011, registrants of Q2 2025

      44.5%

      Open markets look easier than they are

      In Luxembourg, 44.5% of jobs are held by cross-border workers, who appear in no local unemployment count. Vacancies declared to the public agency cover about a quarter of actual hires.

      IGSS, 31 July 2026; ADEM annual report 2025

      29

      Welcome is not the same as work

      Twenty-nine Canadian communities outside Québec take part in federal programmes to attract and welcome francophones. Being listed does not mean jobs are easy to get, or that work is in French: French is the main language at work only in northern New Brunswick.

      IRCC, 2026; 2021 Census

      ≈ 5%

      Moving is rarely the first lever

      Being in the wrong place explains only about 5% of unemployment in the reference study. Changing occupation or widening the search radius usually pays more than changing region. The gain from moving is best established for young graduates.

      Marinescu and Rathelot, 2018 (US data)

      Limits

      What this study cannot tell you

      It describes labour markets, not your individual chances. Occupation often matters more than diploma level, small areas rest on small samples, and definitions differ between countries. Each figure links to its source, period and reliability grade.
